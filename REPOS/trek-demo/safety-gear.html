03 / Medical Telemetry & Load Discipline

How we climb high and come home whole

Acclimatization is math, not bravado. Every departure carries a Garmin inReach satellite messenger, a portable hyperbaric bag on Gokyo, and strict luggage scale enforcement.

01 / Field Medicine Protocol

Four non-negotiable rules above 3,000 m

Protocol A · Telemetry

Twice-daily SpO₂ & resting heart-rate logs

At breakfast (06:30) and before soup (18:30), your lead guide records arterial oxygen saturation and resting pulse in the group ledger alongside the Lake Louise Acute Mountain Sickness score.

Protocol B · Ceiling

400 m sleeping elevation gain limit

Above 3,000 m, our stage plans average under 400 m of net sleeping gain per night, paired with dedicated climb-high, sleep-low acclimatization afternoons at Kyanjin Gompa and Gokyo Third Lake.

Protocol C · Evacuation

Direct helicopter dispatch from Kathmandu

We verify your policy's high-altitude helicopter rescue rider before you board in your home country. In an emergency, we call Fishtail Air or Simrik Air directly from our Kathmandu operations desk.

Protocol D · Porter Welfare

20.0 kg porter cap · Full cold-weather kit

Guided by IPPG standards, no porter carries more than 20.0 kg total. Each guest duffel is capped at 15.0 kg and weighed in Thamel. Every porter receives identical lodge meals, down outerwear, and life insurance.
